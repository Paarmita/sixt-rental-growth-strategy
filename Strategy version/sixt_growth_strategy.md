# Sixt Growth PM — Digital Rent
**Prepared:** May 2026 

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Sixt has achieved three consecutive years of record revenue, crossing €4B for the first time in 2024. Yet a critical growth lever remains underexploited: the digital post-booking journey. Ancillary revenue — from vehicle upgrades, protection products, and add-ons — currently represents **~9.7% of rental revenue (€353.9M)**, below the industry healthy benchmark of **12%** and well short of the **15%** premium-segment target.

Closing that gap represents an **€85–196M annual incremental revenue opportunity**, almost entirely unlockable through two existing digital touchpoints: the **Mobile Check-In (MCI)** funnel and the **Self-Service** product.

This strategy proposes three pillars to close the gap over 24 months:

1. **Widen the Revenue Window** — expand upsell moments across the full post-booking journey, not just the compressed T-1 hour window
2. **Earn the Conversion** — rebuild the MCI funnel to make the right choice the easiest choice, with structured experimentation at every step
3. **Make It Personal** — use available booking signals to serve the right product to the right persona at the right moment

With 80% of Sixt's reservations now made digitally and North America growing at +22.2%, the MCI funnel is the highest-leverage surface available. The imperative is to match that digital reach with digital conversion excellence.

Sixt's current MCI funnel shows products; the strategy below makes it sell them.

## 5. UX Research — Qualitative Insights, Quantitative Signals & Assumptions

### User Personas

#### Persona 1: The Business Traveller ⭐ High Value
**Profile:** Frequent renter, solo, corporate account, time-pressured, loyalty member  
**Behaviour in MCI:** High completion rate; skips protection (covered by corporate card); will upgrade fast if value is obvious; abandons if funnel feels slow  
**Conversion opportunity:** Upgrades (pre-filled, one-tap); skip protection entirely for corporate profiles  
**Design principle:** Speed and pre-fill. Show only what's relevant. Remove friction, not just add choices.

#### Persona 2: The International Tourist ⭐ Highest Protection Potential
**Profile:** Travelling from abroad, unfamiliar with local roads/tolls, high anxiety, often first Sixt rental  
**Behaviour in MCI:** Reads every word of protection screen; overwhelmed by jargon; high abandonment if copy is complex; genuinely needs toll pass and roadside assistance  
**Conversion opportunity:** Protection (plain language + concrete risk framing); toll pass; GPS  
**Design principle:** Reassurance over persuasion. "If the car is stolen, you pay up to €2,800" converts better than "deductible applies."

#### Persona 3: The Family / Road Tripper ⭐ Highest Add-on Potential
**Profile:** 7+ day rental, travelling with family, comfort and reliability matter, practical decision-maker  
**Behaviour in MCI:** Will upgrade if shown in practical terms (seats, luggage capacity — not engine specs); genuinely values fuel service over long trip; high roadside assistance relevance  
**Conversion opportunity:** Upgrades (visual + practical framing); fuel service; roadside assistance  
**Design principle:** Practical, visual, trip-context framing. "Fits 3 suitcases + 2 car seats" > "SUV class."

#### Persona 4: The OTA Booker ✦ Strategic Acquisition Target
**Profile:** Came through Expedia, Kayak, or Booking.com; price-sensitive comparison shopper; low brand affinity; Sixt knows very little about them (no history, no loyalty tier)  
**Behaviour in MCI:** First direct Sixt communication is the MCI trigger email. Likely to skip everything if funnel feels pushy. Responds to bundles and single recommended options over menus  
**Conversion opportunity:** One recommended bundle (not a menu); loyalty enrolment as the post-MCI CTA; convert to direct booker for next rental  
**Design principle:** First impression + loyalty hook. MCI is a customer acquisition moment, not just a upsell moment. "Save 8 minutes next time — create a Sixt account" is the exit CTA.

| Signal | Value | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Digital reservations | 70–80% | High reach — digital is the primary channel |
| App abandonment (industry) | 70% due to friction/unclear pricing | Funnel completion is the primary lever |
| Customers preferring digital check-in (hospitality) | 70% | Demand exists; execution is the constraint |
| McDonald's upsell conversion (visual, no pressure) | 40%+ | Design of the upsell moment matters enormously |

### Key Assumptions *(to validate with internal data)*

1. MCI funnel completion rate is currently below 60% — significant drop-off before payment
2. Protection attach rate in MCI is lower than at the branch counter — digital copy is not converting
3. The majority of MCI upsell revenue comes from T-1 hour; post-booking and Self-Service touchpoints are near zero
4. OTA bookers convert at lower rates than direct bookers in MCI — less trust, less familiarity
5. Business travellers on corporate accounts are shown the same protection screen as leisure customers — a personalisation gap
6. The Self-Service "manage my booking" flow has low engagement for incremental products — most visits are to change dates, not buy extras

## 6. Objectives & 2-Year Winning Aspiration

### Problem Framing

**Three structural problems** prevent the MCI and Self-Service flows from achieving their revenue potential:

**Problem 1 — Single compressed window (Timing)**
Almost all upsell effort is concentrated in the MCI funnel, triggered 1 hour before pickup. This is the worst possible moment for considered purchasing decisions: customers are rushing to the airport, cognitively overloaded, and time-pressured. The post-booking journey has four underused moments of customer attention: immediately after booking, at documentation (T-25h), on-rent, and via Self-Service.

**Problem 2 — Products are presented, not sold (Conversion)**
The current MCI screens show product options with prices and tier names. They do not resolve the customer's underlying question for each product: *"Is this upgrade worth it for my trip?"* (vehicle upgrade), *"Will I actually need this?"* (protection), *"Will this make my trip easier?"* (add-ons). Without answering those questions — through visuals, social proof, concrete risk framing, and smart defaults — customers default to skipping.

**Problem 3 — One size fits all (Relevance)**
A business traveller on a corporate account, a family from the US renting in Europe, and a student booking through Expedia see effectively the same MCI screen. The booking data already available (rental duration, destination, booking source, loyalty tier) would support meaningful differentiation — but it is not being used.

### 2-Year Winning Aspiration

> By end of 2026, Sixt's digital post-booking journey becomes the primary driver of incremental revenue growth — contributing **≥12% of rental revenue in ancillary income** (up from 9.7%), with **IRpR growing +20% YoY** and **MCI completion rate ≥65%**.

This translates to:
- **+€85M minimum** additional annual ancillary revenue (9.7% → 12%)
- **+€196M at full premium target** (12% → 15%)
- MCI evolving from a check-in tool into Sixt's highest-converting upsell surface
- Self-Service evolving from a booking management tool into a persistent revenue re-engagement channel
- OTA bookers being converted to direct, loyalty-enrolled customers through the MCI experience

### Year 1 Goals (Prove it works)
- Grow ancillary revenue from 9.7% → **11%** of rental revenue
- Increase MCI funnel completion rate by **+8pp**
- Increase protection attach rate in MCI by **+15%**
- Increase upgrade attach rate in MCI by **+10%**
- Launch post-booking upsell email touchpoint with ≥20% CTR

### Year 2 Goals (Scale what works)
- Reach **12%** ancillary revenue as % of rental revenue
- Personalisation live for all three primary personas
- Self-Service upsell engagement rate ≥25%
- OTA loyalty enrolment rate via MCI ≥15%
- Biometric / loyalty fast-lane at top 10 airport branches

## 7. High-Level Growth Strategy

### The Three Strategic Pillars

---

#### Pillar 1: Widen the Revenue Window
*"Expand upsell moments from one compressed hour to the full post-booking journey"*

**Root cause addressed:** Timing — insufficient touchpoints to convert customers

**The funnel today vs. target:**

| Journey Stage | Today | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Post-booking (T-0) | Confirmation email only | Upgrade offer + bundle recommendation in email/app |
| Documentation (T-25h) | Admin only (licence upload) | Add-ons surfaced post-documentation completion |
| MCI Funnel (T-1h) | Primary (only) upsell moment | Optimised but no longer sole revenue moment |
| On-rent | No digital touchpoint | Push notification: fuel service, extension, roadside |
| Self-Service | Booking management | Persistent re-engagement for all three product categories |

**Experiments under this pillar:**
- E1: Post-booking upsell email (upgrade offer within 2 hours of booking)
- E2: Documentation-completion add-on prompt (after licence upload)
- E3: On-rent push notification for fuel service / extension upsell
- E4: Self-Service product card redesign for upgrades and protection

**Funnel stage:** Post-booking → Pre-MCI → On-rent

---

#### Pillar 2: Earn the Conversion
*"Make the right choice the easiest choice at every MCI funnel step"*

**Root cause addressed:** Friction and persuasion — existing moments aren't converting well enough

**Key design changes:**
- Smart defaults: pre-select the "recommended" protection tier with one-tap change, not opt-in from zero
- Visual upgrade cards: vehicle photos, practical capacity info (seats, bags), price delta per day — not spec sheets
- Plain-language protection copy: "If the car is scratched, you pay up to €800. This covers you." — not policy language
- Social proof signals: "Most popular among travellers from [country]", "72% of families choose this"
- Progress indicator: show customers how far through the funnel they are to reduce abandonment
- Single recommended bundle for OTA and last-minute bookers: reduce decision fatigue with one curated option

**Experiments under this pillar:**
- E5: Default-on protection A/B test (opt-out vs. opt-in)
- E6: Visual vehicle upgrade card vs. current text-based card
- E7: Social proof label on recommended protection tier
- E8: Single "recommended bundle" screen for OTA bookers vs. standard menu
- E9: Plain-language vs. current protection copy

**Funnel stage:** MCI funnel (all steps) — highest-leverage pillar

---

#### Pillar 3: Make It Personal
*"Show the right product to the right customer at the right moment"*

**Root cause addressed:** Relevance — generic recommendations leave revenue on the table

**Segmentation approach (rule-based, Phase 1):**

| Signal available at booking | Persona inferred | Personalisation action |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate rate code / business booking | Business Traveller | Suppress protection if card coverage detected; lead with upgrade |
| International origin + European destination | International Tourist | Lead with protection (plain language); surface toll pass + GPS |
| Rental duration ≥5 days | Road Tripper | Lead with fuel service; upgrade framing on comfort/reliability |
| OTA booking source (Expedia, Kayak, etc.) | OTA Booker | Single recommended bundle; loyalty enrolment CTA post-MCI |
| First-time Sixt renter | Any persona | Add trust signal ("protected by Sixt"); show branch contact option |
| Repeat renter, previous upgrade | Any persona | Pre-select same upgrade tier; one-tap re-confirm |

**Phase 2 (ML-based, Year 2):** Build recommendation model on conversion signals — which offer converted for which booking attribute combination — and continuously refine sequencing and copy.

**Experiments under this pillar:**
- E10: Business traveller protection suppression test (hide vs. show for corporate accounts)
- E11: Personalised upgrade card copy by rental duration (practical framing for 5+ days vs. aspirational for short trips)
- E12: OTA-specific post-MCI loyalty enrolment screen

**Funnel stage:** Cross-funnel (personalisation layer applied at all steps)

## 8. Detailed Tactics & Experiments

---

### Experiment 1 — Post-Booking Upgrade Email
**Pillar:** Widen the Revenue Window

**Description:** Send a targeted upgrade offer email within 2 hours of reservation completion, while purchase intent is highest. Show two vehicle options above booked class with images, price delta per day, and a one-click accept CTA. A/B test send timing (2h vs. 24h vs. T-48h).

**Hypothesis:** We believe that presenting an upgrade offer immediately after booking — when the customer is still engaged and has already made a purchase decision — will generate upgrade conversions before the compressed T-1h window, because purchase momentum is highest at the point of booking.

**Objective:** Increase upgrade attach rate and distribute upsell across the journey timeline.

**Success metric:** Upgrade email CTR ≥20%; upgrade conversion from email ≥5%; incremental upgrade revenue per email sent.

**Funnel stage:** Post-booking (T-0 to T-24h)

**Expected impact:** Medium — adds a new revenue moment; even 3% email conversion at scale (~49M rental days) represents material incremental revenue.

**Feasibility:** High — requires email template + vehicle inventory API; no new infrastructure.

**Channel:** Email / push notification (app users)

---

### Experiment 2 — Documentation-Completion Add-On Prompt
**Pillar:** Widen the Revenue Window

**Description:** Immediately after a customer completes the documentation step (licence upload + address), show a single contextually relevant add-on offer in-app or via a follow-up push. Destination-aware: toll pass if renting in a toll-heavy market; fuel service if long duration; additional driver if booking name ≠ second traveller signal.

**Hypothesis:** A customer who just completed a task (documentation) is in an active, engaged state — a better moment to introduce an add-on than during the rushed T-1h window.

**Success metric:** Add-on attachment rate from documentation step; comparison vs. same add-on conversion in MCI T-1h funnel.

**Funnel stage:** Post-documentation (T-25h)

**Expected impact:** Medium — context-aware add-ons at a calm moment should outperform the same add-on shown in a rushed funnel.

**Feasibility:** Medium — requires destination-based logic + push/in-app trigger.

**Channel:** In-app prompt + push notification

---

### Experiment 3 — Default-On Protection (Opt-Out vs. Opt-In)
**Pillar:** Earn the Conversion

**Description:** Change the protection plan screen from opt-in (nothing selected, customer must choose) to default-on with the recommended "Smart" tier pre-selected. Customer can downgrade or remove with one tap. Test three variants: (A) current opt-in, (B) default-on Smart, (C) default-on Smart with social proof label ("Most popular — chosen by 68% of renters").

**Hypothesis:** We believe that pre-selecting the recommended protection tier will increase attach rate because the default acts as an implicit recommendation, reducing the cognitive load of making a new purchase decision under time pressure.

**Success metric:** Protection attach rate (primary); protection tier mix (Smart vs. All Inclusive vs. Basic — ensure default doesn't just drive downgrade to Basic); MCI completion rate (guardrail — ensure default doesn't increase abandonment).

**Funnel stage:** MCI — Protection step

**Expected impact:** High — defaults are the highest-leverage single UX change available. Industry evidence: even a neutral default increases selection by 20–30%.

**Feasibility:** High — UI change only; no backend changes required.

**Channel:** MCI funnel (mobile web + app)

**Guardrail:** Monitor protection dispute rate closely — if opt-out default increases disputes, revert immediately.

---

### Experiment 4 — Visual Vehicle Upgrade Card Redesign
**Pillar:** Earn the Conversion

**Description:** Replace current text-based upgrade listing with a visual card format: large vehicle photo, practical capacity info (e.g. "5 seats · 3 large bags · Automatic"), brand name (BMW X1 vs. "SUV Class"), price delta displayed as "€12 more per day" not total cost, and a secondary "Why upgrade?" expandable section. A/B test against current design.

**Hypothesis:** We believe customers skip upgrades because the current presentation does not help them visualise what they gain. Practical, visual framing closes the mental gap between current car and upgrade car faster than spec-based copy.

**Success metric:** Upgrade card CTR; upgrade conversion rate; upgrade revenue per MCI completer.

**Funnel stage:** MCI — Vehicle Selection step

**Expected impact:** High — McDonald's benchmark shows +20% AOV when customers can see what they're choosing. Even +5pp on upgrade conversion at 49M rental days = significant revenue.

**Feasibility:** High — design and copy change; vehicle image library likely already exists.

**Channel:** MCI funnel (mobile web + app)

---

### Experiment 5 — OTA Booker Recommended Bundle Screen
**Pillar:** Make It Personal + Earn the Conversion

**Description:** Detect OTA booking source at MCI entry. Instead of the full vehicle → protection → add-ons step sequence, show OTA bookers a single "Recommended for your trip" bundle screen: one upgrade option, one protection tier, and one contextual add-on, pre-packaged at a bundle price with a saving shown vs. individual prices. Standard multi-step flow available via "Customise instead."

**Hypothesis:** OTA bookers are price-sensitive comparison shoppers with no Sixt loyalty context. A bundle reduces decision fatigue and frames the offer as value rather than three separate upsells. The saving shown on the bundle creates a perceived win.

**Success metric:** Bundle take rate vs. standard flow conversion rate for OTA cohort; total IRpR for OTA cohort vs. control; MCI completion rate for OTA cohort (guardrail).

**Funnel stage:** MCI funnel — all steps (collapsed into one)

**Expected impact:** High strategically — OTA bookers represent a large, underperforming cohort. Even modest conversion improvement on this segment has material revenue impact plus downstream loyalty value.

**Feasibility:** Medium — requires booking source detection + bundle pricing logic; manageable in one sprint.

**Channel:** MCI funnel (mobile web)

---

### Experiment 6 — Post-MCI Loyalty Enrolment CTA (OTA Bookers)
**Pillar:** Make It Personal

**Description:** For OTA bookers who complete MCI, add a post-completion screen: "Great choice, [name]. You just saved 8 minutes at the counter. Create a Sixt account to make your next pickup even faster — and earn points on this rental." CTA: "Join SIXT ONE free." A/B test: (A) no enrolment prompt, (B) enrolment prompt post-MCI, (C) enrolment prompt + retroactive points offer on current rental.

**Hypothesis:** OTA bookers who have just experienced a smooth MCI are at peak positive sentiment toward Sixt. This is the optimal moment to convert them to a direct, loyalty-enrolled relationship — reducing future OTA commission costs and enabling personalisation on subsequent rentals.

**Success metric:** Loyalty enrolment rate from OTA cohort; 90-day direct booking rate for enrolled vs. non-enrolled; LTV delta for enrolled OTA → loyalty member.

**Funnel stage:** Post-MCI completion

**Expected impact:** Medium short-term (low immediate revenue); Very High long-term (each converted OTA → direct customer saves OTA commission cost of ~15–20% per future rental + enables full personalisation).

**Feasibility:** High — CTA screen + loyalty API integration; no new product infrastructure.

**Channel:** MCI post-completion screen + follow-up email

---

### Experiment 7 — Self-Service Product Card Refresh
**Pillar:** Widen the Revenue Window

**Description:** Redesign the "manage my booking" Self-Service product section to surface upgrade and protection cards prominently, with the same visual and copy principles as the redesigned MCI funnel. Add a persistent banner: "Your rental is in X days — add protection now in under 2 minutes." Track engagement and conversion from Self-Service visits, separately from MCI.

**Hypothesis:** Customers who visit Self-Service to manage their booking are actively engaged with their rental. The current Self-Service surface treats incremental products as secondary; making them visible and actionable at this moment captures customers outside the T-1h window who might otherwise skip MCI entirely.

**Success metric:** Self-Service incremental product engagement rate (view + interaction); conversion rate on protection and upgrades from Self-Service; % of rentals with incremental product added via Self-Service (currently near zero — any conversion is incremental).

**Funnel stage:** Self-Service (pre-rental, any time after booking)

**Expected impact:** Medium — currently near-zero Self-Service conversion on incremental products means any improvement is pure gain. Sized at 10% engagement × 5% conversion = meaningful IRpR contribution.

**Feasibility:** High — UI/copy change on existing surface.

**Channel:** Web + app (manage my booking section)

### Experimentation Framework

**Approach:** All experiments run as controlled A/B tests with statistical significance threshold of 95% and minimum sample size calculated per experiment. Prioritise MCI funnel tests (high-volume, fast-cycling) before Self-Service tests (lower volume, longer run time needed).

**Guardrails applied to every experiment:**
- MCI completion rate must not fall more than 2pp below baseline
- Protection dispute rate monitored weekly
- Post-rental NPS tracked for any test cohort

**Holdout group:** Maintain 10% global holdout (no experiments) to measure cumulative experiment impact on revenue vs. clean baseline.

**Learning cadence:** Weekly experiment review; monthly strategy review against north star metrics; quarterly roadmap re-prioritisation based on learnings.
